Research overview · Video generation × physical computation

Give the model a physical workspace.

Translate a video generator’s internal features into an executable 3D scene, compute its physical consequences, and return those consequences to guide generation.

ArchitectureTrainingReferences & alternatives

The problem. A plausible-looking video can still contain broken contacts, changing shapes or disappearing objects. A physics simulator can compute consistent motion, but it needs an explicit scene: which objects exist, their geometry, their state and how they interact.

Our approach. Learn that scene from the observed video and the generator’s own interpretation. A learned adapter connects visual understanding to physical computation; a shared conditioning pathway connects the resulting motion back to the video model.

A route from the model’s own understanding to explicit physical computation, without a person supplying a ready-made 3D scene.

What updating could add

A physical workspace that responds to new information during generation—and lets us inspect how the model’s scene hypothesis evolves.

How the adapter learns to make useful scenes

We compare two scene representations—one that predicts the scene globally, and one that maintains a separate, persistent representation for each object. Each is trained with either scene-and-motion supervision alone or an additional video-generation objective. The simulator and the way its outputs reach the generator are held constant.

Learn objects and state

Training examples supervise geometry, identity and physical state where labels are available. Visual shape and simplified collision geometry have different roles: the latter must preserve the surfaces needed for contact.

In the generation-aware variants, the video model’s training error also flows back through the conditioning pathway and differentiable simulation parameters into the adapter. The generator’s weights remain frozen.

The scene reader sees only permitted observations and generator features from a proposal made without the target future. Future training video supplies a learning target on a separate prediction branch; future evaluation video is never an input. Discrete object assignments and geometry construction need not be differentiable.

What this line of work could contribute

From internal features to computation

The aim is to turn information inside a generator into an executable physical model that helps that same generator. This would connect learned visual interpretation to explicit physical consequences, reducing dependence on someone supplying a simulation-ready scene.

From a fixed plan to an evolving workspace

The proposed extension rereads internal features at selected generation stages, revises the scene hypothesis and recomputes its future. It could correct an initially mistaken interpretation while exposing how the physical hypothesis changes. Whether this helps enough to justify its added computation remains open.

What would count as progress?

The output must improve, not just the simulation: more accurate motion and contacts, preserved object identity and shape, less flicker, and retained appearance. The current study compares adapter designs against generation with the controller off. Establishing the value of the generator’s features also needs a matched scene builder without those features. The continual version needs a direct comparison with the fixed version, including runtime.

A physically valid simulation does not force every generated pixel to obey it. Scene reconstruction and the return pathway can both fail. The workspace is an inspectable physical hypothesis decoded from the model—not a complete or proven record of its reasoning. These are intended contributions and open comparisons, not claims of demonstrated superiority.

References & competing approaches

A selective reading map, from object-based physical prediction to simulation-guided video and latent physical representations. These papers establish the surrounding building blocks; they do not establish that our particular adapter or continual-workspace proposal is better.

Start here: PhysGen for simulation-to-video, PHANTOM for latent physical dynamics, and Video-Coder for an executable scene-building agent.

1. Objects and external workspaces

  • Interaction NetworksBattaglia et al. · 2016

    Predicts physical dynamics through objects and their relationships in a graph. A foundation for our interaction representation; it is a learned dynamics model, not a video diffusion generator.

  • Visual SketchpadHu et al. · 2024

    Lets multimodal language models draw and reason over visual artifacts. It motivates an inspectable workspace, but demonstrates reasoning in language models, not a 3D workspace inside video denoising.

2. Connecting motion and simulation to video

  • PhysDreamerZhang et al. · 2024

    Uses a video model’s dynamics prior to infer interactive physical behavior for 3D objects. Important precedent: letting a generator inform simulator parameters is already an established direction.

  • MOFA-VideoNiu et al. · 2024

    Converts sparse controls into motion fields and uses warped image features to guide a frozen video model. It addresses the motion-to-appearance connection, without itself recovering a physical scene.

  • PhysGenLiu et al. · 2024

    Combines image understanding, rigid-body simulation and diffusion-based refinement. This is a direct predecessor for the overall pipeline; our proposed distinction concerns how the scene is inferred from the generator and trained for downstream use.

  • Wan-MoveChu et al. · 2025

    Propagates first-frame features along dense point trajectories to guide generation. It supplies motion-control precedent and a backbone for this implementation; a trajectory controller alone does not determine which motion is physically correct.

3. Physical information inside generators

  • VideoREPAZhang et al. · 2025

    Aligns a video generator’s token relations with a video-understanding model. It is an alternative way to improve physical generation through representations, without executing an explicit simulator.

  • ProprioHassan et al. · 2026

    Uses a frozen generator’s flow residuals for self-scoring, selection and refinement. It motivates making internal signals actionable; our route instead decodes a scene and computes its physical consequences.

  • The Invisible Hand of PhysicsEsmati et al. · 2026

    Finds decodable plausibility signals along inverted real-video trajectories. This supports investigating internal representations, while reminding us that reconstruction-path probes are not evidence of reliable scene inference during free generation.

  • Does Physics Live in the Activations?Kneifl et al. · 2026

    Finds physical quantities readable from object-associated diffusion features, including early denoising stages, and explores steering. It motivates the adapter’s input; accurate physical readouts do not by themselves establish complete 3D reconstruction.

4. The closest architectural alternatives

  • PHANTOMShen et al. · 2026

    Jointly models visual generation and physics-aware latent dynamics. The distinction we want to test is an explicit, executable physical scene with inspectable states and rules, rather than a learned physical embedding alone.

  • Video-CoderWang et al. · 2026

    Trains an agent to write and execute dynamic 3D scene code that guides downstream video generation. Our adapter instead starts from the target generator’s internal features and observed geometry. Whether this foundation is better requires a matched comparison.

Our research question combines these threads: can the target generator’s own features support a useful executable scene, and does refreshing that scene during denoising help beyond building it once? The continual version is proposed. This reading map is not a claim of novelty, a head-to-head benchmark, or an exhaustive survey. Links checked 24 November 2026.
